Uneven Share of Manufacturing Jobs Across India Points to Regional Gaps

India’s manufacturing employment varies sharply across states and UTs, reflecting differences in regional economic structures, urbanisation, and the balance between agriculture, services and manufacturing.

By Sanvi Awasthi · 20/09/26

Manufacturing accounts for different share of employment across India, but a latest observation of the SDG 9 dataset showed striking differences between states and union territories.

The data from Mospi measures manufacturing employment as a proportion of total employment, it showed Dadra & Nagar Haveli at the top with 62.26%, followed by the Andaman & Nicobar Islands at 21.91%, Manipur at 21.47%, Punjab at 20.64% and Gujarat at 20.48%. At the bottom were Meghalaya with 3.08%, followed by Nagaland at 3.60% and Ladakh at 4.05%.

The main finding

Distribution of manufacturing across India

Graph 1 highlights the states and UTs where manufacturing constitutes the largest share of employment. It shows that the highest shares are not confined to India's largest states.

The results show differences in the constitution of employment, rather than a difference in the absolute number of manufacturing jobs. The variation also changes over time. The Andaman & Nicobar Islands recorded a 15.78-percentage-point increase between the first and latest observation, whereas Chandigarh fell by 5.30 points and Delhi by 3.42 points.

The reporting question

. The analysis shows that differences exist and leads to the bigger question of how can they be overcome?

What the data shows

In 2025, manufacturing accounted for 62.26% of employment in Dadra & Nagar Haveli, compared with 12.14% nationally — a gap of 50.12 percentage points.

What the data does not prove

The data shows the proportion of employment in manufacturing, not the total number or quality of manufacturing jobs. It does not prove that states with higher shares have more jobs, better employment opportunities, higher wages, greater industrial output, or faster economic growth. It also cannot establish why regional differences exist.

About the data

Methodology and limitations

Data was sourced from the Ministry of Statistics and Programme Implementation (MoSPI). It measures manufacturing employment as a proportion of total employment by state, area and gender. The dataset was cleaned and grouped for comparison. It does not measure total jobs, wages or industrial output, and differences may reflect varying employment structures.
